# Why the correct structure sits below rank 1

Generated by `tools/audit_isomer_discrimination.py`. Read-only.

## Truth-control recovery

Of 92 truth controls:

| Outcome | Controls |
|---|---|
| Truth at rank 1 | 44 |
| Truth absent from the top 10 | 24 |
| Truth below rank 1, same formula as rank 1 | 19 |
| Truth below rank 1, different formula from rank 1 | 4 |
| Truth below rank 1, truth row carries no evidence score | 1 |

The rows are exclusive and an unscored truth row is reported under that heading even when it also shares the rank-1 formula, which is why the same-formula total below is 20 rather than 19.

20 of the 24 controls whose truth sits below rank 1 share the rank-1 candidate's molecular formula (83%), the 1 unscored one included. For those the mass and formula axes are degenerate: every candidate fits the same measured mass equally well, so no mass gate, tolerance change or formula prior can separate them. The failure is isomer discrimination, which is a different population from the rank-1 mass mismatches in `docs/pipeline_refinement_handoff.md` item 2.

7 of them trail rank 1 by less than 0.05 of `candidate_confidence`.

## Is rank 1 ever an arbitrary pick?

On 3 of 92 truth controls (3.3%) rank 1 is tied with at least one lower-ranked candidate on every displayed evidence column (mean tie 2.33, largest 3). In 1 the truth is inside that tied group but not first, so the ordering that decided the report was made by `rank_frame`'s final tie-break on `predicted_smiles`, not by evidence.

## The spectral axis

`forward_spectrum_score` compares predicted fragments to observed ions and predicted losses to observed losses. Of 61 truth controls with a local observed spectrum, **56 have zero same-axis agreement for every candidate in the top 10** — the axis contributes nothing at all to those rankings.

A fragment of mass `m` and a loss of `parent - m` are the same event. Pooling the two axes and allowing a hydrogen shift revives 56 of those 56: at least one observed peak becomes explainable.

That is a real defect, but pooling alone is **not** a ranking fix. Re-ordering the delivered top 10 by each normalization of the pooled score, against a delivered baseline of 37 at rank 1 and 52 within the top 3:

| Variant | Rank 1 | Top 3 | Moved up | Moved down | Δ rank 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `raw_count` | 18 | 37 | 10 | 31 | -19 |
| `recall` | 18 | 37 | 10 | 31 | -19 |
| `f1_recall_precision` | 18 | 37 | 10 | 33 | -19 |
| `geometric_mean` | 15 | 36 | 10 | 37 | -22 |
| `recall_over_log_npredicted` | 15 | 35 | 10 | 36 | -22 |

Every variant loses. The bond-breaking fragmenter in `nce/evidence.py::candidate_fragment_bins` breaks only acyclic single bonds and is blind to regiochemistry, so it cannot rank isomers even when its output is compared on the right axis. Fixing the axis makes the signal visible; it does not make it discriminating.

## What this rules out

- A looser or tighter mass window. Every candidate in a degenerate group fits the same mass.
- Backfilling `evidence_confidence` onto unscored rows. It is worth doing for correctness, but it is the operative cause for exactly 1 of 24 controls, and it hands the unscored row the *same* score as its competitors rather than a distinguishing one.
- Retention index. `docs/paper/data/retention/audit_rt_feasibility.json` measures decoy AUC 0.651 overall and 0.590 for lib 305, where the proxy correlation is negative (r = -0.765, n = 13).
